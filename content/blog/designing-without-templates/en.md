Most student portfolios resemble the same three templates: a large hero, three project cards, a skill cloud, and a footer. It works. It also disappears in a recruiter’s stack of tabs.

## Start with one composition

Before components, I decide what the first viewport should feel like. For this site that meant:

- a name large enough to own the screen
- a quiet gradient atmosphere
- almost nothing else competing

If the first screen could belong to anyone after removing the navigation, the branding is too weak.

## Constraints that help

I keep a short list of hard rules while designing:

- **Typography first.** Layout follows the type scale, not the other way around.
- **No decorative cards by default.** Cards appear when they help interaction or scanning.
- **Motion with purpose.** Entrances and hovers - not parallax everywhere.
- **One job per section.** About explains who I am. Projects show the work. Contact closes.

## The trade-off

Handcrafting takes longer than cloning a template. The upside is that recruiters remember something - a color, a title size, a sense of care. That memory is the point of a portfolio.
