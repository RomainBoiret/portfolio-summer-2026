I still rebuild classic browser games. Not because the world needs another Snake - because small games force clear thinking.

## Why games work as practice

A game loop is honest. Either the paddle hits the ball or it does not. That clarity teaches faster than another half-finished dashboard.

Rebuilding **Snake**, **Pong**, and **Simon** pushed me to own:

- input handling without a framework
- frame-friendly updates
- collision and scoring as plain data
- feedback that feels immediate

## What transfers to product work

The same instincts show up in application UI:

- **State should stay simple.** Prefer explicit values over clever mutation.
- **Feedback is part of the feature.** A clear hover or error beats a silent failure.
- **Finish the loop.** Ship a small complete experience before expanding scope.

## Why this belongs on a portfolio

Recruiters may not play the games. They notice that you can start something, structure it, and ship it. For a student, that signal matters more than another unfinished product clone.
